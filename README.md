# Isoline

Topographic patterns, palettes and design tokens. Static site: open `index.html` or serve the folder (`python3 -m http.server`).

Live at **https://masterclaudecoder.github.io/isoline/**. Every push to the main branch deploys it through GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`). The workflow also captures previews for new libraries and commits them to `previews/`. Pages has to be switched on once in the repository settings (Settings, Pages, Source: GitHub Actions).

## Pages
A loading screen covers slow starts (it only appears if loading takes more than a beat). When the page is ready, the logo rushes toward you and blurs away while a hole opens from its middle and widens until the page is uncovered. The home page keeps its last drawing of your design, so the Studio card shows at once. On a first visit it's drawn while the loading screen is still up, so the entrance plays on an idle page; later changes redraw it after the entrance. The video encoder library loads only when you first export an MP4. The studio waits for its first drawing before showing, and a shared design link is drawn straight away behind it instead of cross-fading over whatever was open last.

The home page (`#/`) shows three tools as large cards. Clicking the logo there rolls a new look for everything: a fresh palette, a new Studio design and a new Components kit, both in that palette. As when a look is applied in the Studio, each card keeps a still copy of its old picture on top while the new one is made, then all three copies fade off together, so nothing flashes and the cards change as one. A small "Rolling a new look" note by the logo shows it's working. The first click only nudges the logo (tipping it right), so a stray click changes nothing; from the second click on, every click rolls again. Every roll is kept: undo and redo buttons appear beside the logo (and `⌘Z` / `⇧⌘Z` work on the home page), so a look rolled past by accident comes back, with its palette, design and kit together. Moving between pages is one continuous transition: the top bar stays put, pages crossfade, a tool's preview morphs into the tool (each stripe of the home palette travels and stretches into its own column), and floating bars pull up from the bottom once their page lands.
- **Studio** (`#/studio`): the pattern, gradient, effects and motion editor described below. An orange button pinned to the foot of each panel moves on to the next step.
- **Palettes** (`#/palettes`):
  - Space generates new colors for every unlocked column, using OKLCH harmonies (auto, analogous, monochrome, complement, triad, split, pastel, vivid, earthy).
  - New colors sweep across the columns as a wave, with the hex codes rolling through the in-between values.
  - The harmony button shows the chosen harmony as color dots. Its menu lists every harmony with a sample drawn from your palette's hue, and a tooltip says what each one does.
  - V (Vary) nudges colors instead of replacing them, and the proportions toggle sizes the columns 60-30-10.
  - Columns show their nearest name and contrast, and can be locked, edited, shaded, added or removed. Drag a grip to reorder: the column follows the pointer and the others slide aside. New columns grow in, removed ones fold away, and sorting or undo glides each color to its new place.
  - Export copies hex codes, CSS variables, JSON or a share link (`#/palettes/2f6fdb-...`), or downloads a PNG. **Use in Studio** applies the palette there, and its arrow offers **Use in Builder**.
  - The bar is smoked, frosted glass with white text, so it stays legible over any color. In Chromium browsers its rim also bends what's behind it.
- **Components** (`#/components`) has two modes:
  - **Libraries:** a directory of about 120 UI, headless, animation, effects, icon, chart, form, 3D, styling and font libraries, filterable by framework, category and search. Each card is a window onto the site. At deploy time `tools/previews.mjs` saves each library's own OpenGraph banner, or a screenshot of its page when it has none, into `previews/` (kept in the repository). Copies without those files (like the single-file build) fall back to the [11ty OpenGraph image service](https://www.11ty.dev/docs/services/opengraph/), then to a pane drawn from the site's icon. Picks come first, then one section per category.
  - **Builder:** design tokens for type, color, spacing, radius, elevation and motion, plus editable design rules. Every token tile shows the same small card from your kit (a heading, two lines and a button on a surface); hovering animates only the property that token controls, in the same rhythm on every tile: a smaller value, a larger one, then yours. Type resizes the heading itself, so the lines beside it make way; color blends to the dark scheme and on to another palette; artwork pans the background, spacing changes the room at the sides (the text stays at its height), radius the corners, elevation the lift, motion slides the card out and back with your own duration and easing, and rules write the card in part by part. Fonts are picked from searchable menus that show each font in its own face, group a catalog by kind and suggest what pairs with the other font. You can also type any Google or installed font, or upload your own files. The preview is a made-up document review product, so every component shows real content: a comment thread, a command menu, a form, a documents table, a chart titled by what it says, notices and controls. Four pages are each built for one task: a home page whose first screen holds the product itself, the editor with a comment thread in its margin on the artwork, in one of two hero layouts picked beside the page picker: beside the headline, running off the page's edge, or below, with the headline and buttons on the left, the lead on the right and the editor across the page under them. Either way the headline's room is measured in its own font, so a wide or large display face gets the width it needs instead of breaking into a narrow stack. Then come three spreads that each show the editor with its margin on one part of the work (a suggestion to accept or reject, who has signed off with a reminder for whoever hasn't, two drafts compared word by word) on the artwork or a tint of the accent, and a closing section with a working Google Doc import. Everything in them can be clicked. On phones the home page is laid out for the hand: the editor runs edge to edge under the headline, the three spreads become a row of cards you swipe through, the menu opens from a full-size button, and the call to action rises in at the bottom once you scroll past the first one. Then an article, an app settings page and a pricing comparison. They avoid template tells (no badge over the headline, no invented stats, logo strips or icon tiles, one picture per idea). The made-up product is called Margin; its mark is a lowercase m drawn as two arches whose last leg stops short and ends in a note in the accent. The mark stands on the wordmark's baseline, so the logo lines up with the title and with buttons beside it in any font. The logo remix on the home page keeps the type scale at 1.25 or below. The preview is a page of a real size: it fits the window by default, takes any size preset from the same size control as the Studio, or resizes by dragging its edges, and every page adapts from a phone to a wide display. Colors start from Isoline's own, the palette tool or a saved palette, mapped to page, surface, text and accent roles with a neutral, tinted or bold strength, and Edit in Palettes round-trips them. Artwork can bring in the Studio's current design behind the product picture and as the article cover. Motion covers duration, easing, and how things appear and disappear with a stagger, replayable on the preview. Until you change a color, the kit wears Isoline's own colors and follows the app's theme and accent, and the home page card shows the landing page in a browser window tilted back in space, with the same page on a phone in front casting its shadow on it. The scene tilts toward the pointer over the card. Export copies CSS variables, a Tailwind v4 theme, JSON tokens, or a design or build brief for AI agents.

## Tutorial
Each page has a short tutorial the first time you visit it. Home walks through the three tools and the logo remix, then the dot clicks into the Studio, whose guided tour follows. Palettes and Components get guided tours of their own. (The Studio's earlier tutorial pane, four animated scenes on a miniature Studio, is switched off for now; `PANE` in `tour.js` brings it back.) In a tour, a spotlight glides from part to part, the dot goes there first and clicks what the step opens, and a note beside it explains. The pointer is the app's orange dot with a white ring. It moves on spring-eased, gently curved paths (`SPRING` in `tour.js` switches back to the earlier cubic easing) and ripples where it clicks; short hops to a small target, like the logo, ease in without overshooting. A step that needs a certain state sets it first (the Components tour switches to Libraries for its Libraries steps, also when you go back from the Builder), a step whose part isn't on screen at that width is skipped, the spotlight follows the page while it scrolls, and leaving the page ends its tour. Tours fit phone screens too. When a tour ends, the dot spirals into the Settings button in a puff of Surprise sparks. Arrow keys step through, Esc ends it, and **Settings → Show the tutorial** replays the one for the page you're on. Tutorials don't open for shared links or under automation.

## Agents
**Share → Copy for agents** in the top bar copies the agent guide. Page briefs with values and settings come from `Isoline.brief()` and from Export in Components. Agents can drive the whole app through `window.Isoline`: `studio`, `palettes`, `components`, `color`, `go` and `guide()`. The guide is also embedded in the page as `#isoline-agent-guide`.

## Interface
Preset and effect panels have a search field: every word typed has to match a name, a group or a word people use for the look (blur finds depth of field, print finds halftone and riso). Cards ease back in as they match, and when nothing does, a note says so and offers to clear the search or show every group. Settings with a hint carry a small i beside their name; hover it or the name to read it. Everything animates: menus, popovers and dialogs fade in and out, rows and sections fold open and closed, segmented controls slide their selection, lists glide when they reorder, and theme changes crossfade. Every surface shares one hover. Tooltips are custom and appear after a short pause. In the studio, the canvas bar can fold the side columns away (as in the builder) or show the canvas full screen (`F`, `Esc` to leave).

## How it works
The left column is the build, made of one slot per layer: **Preset, Generator, Shape, Lines, Colors, Objects, Effects, Motion**. Click a slot to open its options in the middle column. Two or more effects or objects fold into a deck of cards with their names beside it, so the column stays short. The deck keeps one width however many cards it holds: cards further back are smaller and fainter. Clicking a card spreads the deck and opens that card's settings, cards slide in and out as they're added and removed, and Fold gathers them up again. Every choice there has a live preview of your current design. Options are grouped into collapsible sections that remember whether you left them open, and a closed group shows a short summary of its values. Groups and settings explain themselves on hover, and shape, line and gradient settings also play a short loop of your design swept from low to high. Settings a generator ignores are hidden or dimmed with the reason. The Motion tile plays a small loop of your design when motion is on. Effects stack: `+ Add effect` adds another, the count badge shows how many there are, drag the grip to reorder them, and × removes one.

## Generators
- **Canvas:** drag the handles that appear near the canvas edges to resize it. Your colors overlap when they run out of room, wrap only when they truly don't fit, and drag to reorder. Edit in Palettes opens them in the palette tool, and its button brings them back.
- **Feedback:** Settings, Send feedback opens a filled-in GitHub issue (with optional page details) for you to post. To file issues straight from the app instead, deploy the small worker in `worker/` and set `FEEDBACK_URL` in `app.js`. It keeps the GitHub token on the server.
- **Contours** (vector, exports SVG; coming from a gradient, they start as bands in its colors): Blobs, Terrain, Flow, Rings, Ridges, Dunes, Lines, Strata, Waves. Scale, detail, warp, liquify, angle and metaball points. Double-click the canvas to add a peak, then drag to move it or scroll to resize it.
- **Mesh:** draggable color points blended in OKLab.
- **Flow:** domain-warped simplex noise for soft, flowing color fields. Pair it with Grain.
- **Noise:** clouds, ridges, billow, cells, veins, marble, neuro, smoke ring and smoke (curling veils, made to sit under the glyph field), with scale, detail, stretch, angle, warp and contrast. A setting a noise type ignores stays in place, dimmed, with a tooltip saying so.
- **Aura:** after the reactive background of Yandex Music's My Wave. Two to four soft blobs of color are stacked over your darkest color, each smaller, lighter and a little off-center from the one behind it, their outlines bulging and folding as they morph out of step with each other. Light streams out from the back blob in soft rays, and an optional faint outline traces each blob. Size, wobble, edge, layers, rays, outline and color turn.
- **Wave:** after Framer's Wave Gradient shader, the background of caddis.app. Four colors in two soft crossing blends are read through a field a sine wave twists, so broad ribbons of color fold through each other. Angle, frequency, ripples, twist, softness and blend; moving, the wave travels a whole number of periods per loop. The Caddis preset uses the settings and colors caddis.app does.
- **Linear, Radial, Conic** gradients.
- **Image:** your own picture, filling the canvas, as a base for effects. It is kept in the browser outside the design state (one at a time), so undo history and saved settings stay small.

## Objects
Objects sit on top of any generator. Add up to eight: a sphere, torus, cube, capsule, blob, pyramid or diamond, raymarched and lit from the upper left, or a picture card. Each sits under the effects (they apply to it) or over them, and drag their grips to change which draws on top. Each has its own material:
- **Shaded:** lit through your colors.
- **Design:** wraps your current design round the shape, the whole picture across each side, so it turns with the form instead of lining up with what's behind it. It reads the design from before any objects, so one object never wears another.
- **Texture:** for Design and Preset surfaces, Wrap puts one copy on each side, Front projects it straight through, and Globe wraps it around like a map, mirrored front and back so it never shows a seam. Scale, rotate and shift the picture (past its edges it repeats mirrored), and soften the edges between sides with Edge blend.
- **Preset:** wraps a preset round it, projected from three sides so there's no seam (faceted forms take one projection per face, so nothing doubles up). **Vantablack** is the first preset in the list: flat black that swallows the light, with a hairline of sheen on the rim, like the black shapes in Alfa-Bank's ads.
- **Glass:** bends what's behind it most at the edges, like a liquid glass pill, with refraction, frost, dispersion, rim light and a tint.
- Pictures show as a photo, recolored through your palette, or as plain Vantablack cards.

Drag an object on the canvas to move it and the white handle to resize it. Three rings turn it: the blue circle rotates it in the picture's plane, the red upright ring tilts it toward or away from you and the green flat ring spins it (hold Shift to snap to 15°). Dragging while the loop plays is drawn by the loop itself, so the edit never flickers against the motion. Placement, orientation (tilt and spin) and surface (shine, ripple, rounding, shadow) are in its panel, along with duplicate and remove. Drift turns objects, Flow bobs them and Pulse beats them. Each object can stay still, or follow the loop at its own pace, from 5 times slower in reverse to 5 times faster (whole steps, so it still loops). Designs made with the old 3D shape generator open as a gradient with the same shape as an object.

## Colors
**Your colors** come first: a background plus up to six inks for contours, or up to eight stops for gradients. With more than two inks, plain contours cycle through them level by level, and blended or layered contours run a smooth ramp through them. Below that are palette tabs: Suggested (harmonies from your strongest color, plus the closest library palette), Curated, Brands (including Russian ones: Yandex Neo, the My Wave glow; Yandex; Yandex Go, the taxi yellow with black and white; Yandex Intel, the Alice AI violet; Alfa, Sber, VTB, T-Bank, Ozon, Wildberries, MTS, VK, Avito, MegaFon, Kaspersky), and Saved (from the palette tool). Colors that match a library palette show its name. Pages of nine cards turn from the pager underneath, and Sort switches between color order (neutrals first, then around the hue wheel) and name. Every palette card is resampled to your number of colors and applies at that count.

The color picker has two modes: a color field, or sliders for the chosen format (HEX, RGB, HSL, HSB, OKLCH). Move toward its top-right corner to switch modes. It shows the nearest color name, from the "best of" list of [color-name-list](https://github.com/meodai/color-name-list) (MIT). You can paste any of those formats into its fields. Print inks only appear when an ink-only print effect is in the stack.

## Motion
The Motion slot makes the design loop without a visible seam:
Contour levels are spread over a range sampled from the canvas plus a wide margin and, for Flow and Pulse, from points all round the loop. A peak growing or crossing the edge never re-spaces the whole map, and the still frame matches the playing ones. Breathe slides the levels, and each band keeps its color as it travels.
- **Drift:** the field glides round a circle; on Mesh, each color point wanders its own loop.
- **Flow:** the shape keeps morphing along a closed path through the noise at an even pace, so it never visibly reverses or hurries, and flows back into where it started. Linear, radial and conic blends stream instead: their colors run along the blend without end, so radial rings keep moving outward.
- **Breathe:** contour lines swell outward. Blends sway their colors in and out, and mesh, flow and noise fields swell gently from the middle.
- **Pulse:** four soft beats per loop push the field out from the center while it morphs a little.
- **Endless (Flow only):** the morph follows a path built from turns at irrational rates, slower than the main one so the pace never stalls, so it never closes and never repeats. Time runs on unbounded in the preview and through the whole length of an export, eased once per loop length by the easing curve. It doesn't loop, so only Once playback is offered while it's on.
- **Unbound (with Endless):** the path also travels steadily on through the pattern, faster than it circles, so the morph never even revisits similar shapes.

Hovering a motion type plays it on your own design in the tooltip, and hovering a playback, direction or easing shows its timing as a moving dot. Editing while it plays pauses the loop, lets the edit land, then crossfades the loop back in from its start.

Each motion has a loop length, amplitude and seed, plus loop, ping-pong or once playback, forward or reverse, and easing. The easing is a preset or a custom cubic bezier you drag. Effects with randomness have their own seed. Play previews the loop on the canvas. Playback draws contours at the size they appear on screen. Frames are timed by how long the GPU takes to draw them, so a background tab or a pause never counts as slow. If they really are slow the preview gets lighter twice, then a note offers to remove effects or stop motion, once per visit. Exports always render every frame at full quality. Video is a format in the Export menu, alongside SVG and PNG, and every export shows its progress. **Export MP4** renders every frame at an exact timestamp using the browser's video encoder (H.264, or VP9 where H.264 isn't available) and [mp4-muxer](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mp4-muxer) (MIT). Browsers without WebCodecs record a WebM in real time. GIF export has an **Animated WebP** switch: every frame keeps its full color with no palette banding, usually in a smaller file. It shows wherever the browser can encode WebP.

## Lines (contours only)
Contours draw on the GPU while previewing and playing, so dense bands stay smooth in motion. While drifting, the grid snaps to whole cells of the field and the levels come from the whole area the loop travels over, so lines glide instead of shivering. Exports still use the vector path where it applies.

Lines (strokes), Bands (filled stripes, PewDiePie-style), Layers (stacked fills) or Gradient (bands shaded through the inks on either side of them). Count or gap, bias, weight, swell, taper, accent lines, smoothing and cleanup.

## Effects (WebGL2, run top to bottom)
- **Distort:** liquify, waves, twirl, bulge, ripples (optionally animated, rings travelling outward a whole number of times per loop), water (looking down through a moving, sunlit surface, computed exactly from its waves so it stays light: swells and chop bend what's beneath, light focused by those same waves slides over the floor, so it never repeats, and an optional depth tint; every wave moves a whole number of turns per loop), caustic web (the bright cell-edge web that used to be water, optionally animated), kaleidoscope
- **Glass** (iOS-style bezel refraction, frost, hairline highlights and slight dispersion, with rounded glass blocks): fluted glass, glass blocks, liquid glass
- **Light:** progressive blur, glow, relief, god rays, vignette
- **Color:** gradient map, posterize, heatmap, liquid metal (lit from a smoothed height map, so edges stay clean)
- **On-canvas handles:** effects with a center (god rays' light, liquid glass, the depth of field focus, twirl, bulge, ripples) show a handle on the canvas while their panel is open. Drag it, or press anywhere to move it there. Second handles set the radius or size, the in-focus area and falloff, and a focus band's angle (Shift snaps to 15°).
- **Depth of field:** the picture stays sharp around a focal point you place (or along a band through it, like a tilt-shift lens) and softens with distance, with bokeh that opens highlights into discs. Progressive blur and depth of field read a mipmapped copy of the picture, so even 200px blurs stay smooth instead of splitting lines.
- **Print:** halftone and CMYK halftone, engrave, risograph, dither. Halftone dots grow from nothing at white, meet their neighbours at mid grey and close into solid ink at black: circles touch at 50% and merge, squares stand on their corners so 50% is an exact checkerboard, and triangles alternate up and down so they tile the plane outright. Dots sit on a square or hexagonal grid, and squares, triangles and stars can each take a random turn. Four switches change how it sizes dots, all off by default except Merge: **Size by tone** (in color, dots follow lightness as ink only does, instead of staying large), **Even sizes** (every shape covers the same share of its cell for a given tone), **Merge when dark** (off, dots stop growing when they touch, so they keep their shape), and **One-way triangles** (ink triangles all point one way; past mid grey, paper triangles pointing the other way shrink to nothing; set the angle to 90° for sideways rows).
- **Stylize:** pixels, crystallize, ASCII, glyph field (a random character in every cell, its opacity following the brightness underneath, optionally reshuffling a whole number of times per loop), scanlines, chromatic. ASCII and the glyph field draw in Geist Mono, the interface font or the system monospace
- **Texture:** paper, film grain (optionally animated, a fresh pattern every frame)
- **Interactive:** click ripples (rings spread from where you click and die away) and drag trails, run on a real fluid simulation (stable fluids with vorticity): Fluid carries the picture along with the flow like paint and lets it settle back, Smoke leaves curling smoke without touching the picture. Trails have no length limit and fade by their life. They answer the pointer on the canvas and in HTML exports, and their tiles show a click and a drag frozen part-way

## Workflow
- The bar under the canvas holds undo, redo, **Variation** (`R`: new seed and small nudges, same look) and **Surprise** (`⇧R`). Surprise mixes recipes: a whole preset, a preset's shape in a random palette with another preset's effects, or a look built from scratch on any generator, now and then with an object or two on top. It always crossfades. Each roll is first drawn small off screen and timed. A look whose full frame would take more than about 1.5 seconds is skipped and another rolled (the tests get a second in all). The home logo's remix uses the same guard.
- **Notifications** stack in the bottom right (along the bottom on phones), pause while hovered, and carry an Undo where one helps.
- **First load** waits for the fonts, then the page rises in, so nothing flashes or shifts.
- A dice in each panel randomizes just that layer.
- Motion closes the build list as a wide tile playing the loop.
- **Safe values** (in settings): keeps everything in ranges that render cleanly. **Unlock values** lets you type past slider limits, after you confirm.
- **Designs** (top bar, `⌘S`): save designs to this browser, as many separate ones as you like (**Save as new design** starts another). Each keeps its earlier versions too, up to 30. Open, rename or delete one (with undo). Import or export one design or all of them as JSON. Every file carries the app version it was saved with, so files from an older or newer Isoline say what may look different before they open.
- **Share** (top bar): **Copy link** puts the whole design in the URL, to bookmark or send, and **Copy for agents** copies the guide an AI agent needs to drive Isoline. Share, Designs and Export dim and soften the page behind them while open.
- Click any value to type it. Settings import and export as JSON. `⌘Z` / `⇧⌘Z` undo and redo.
- **Export:** SVG for contours, PNG 1–4× for everything, MP4, GIF or animated WebP for motion, HTML (a standalone page that plays the design live, fills the window and answers clicks and drags), or `C` to copy for Figma.
- **Theme:** Auto, light or dark, plus an accent color, in settings.
- **Lag warning:** when motion plays below the frame rate set in Settings (60 fps by default; 45, 30 or off), a note offers to remove effects or stop motion. Closing it quiets it for that look only, so another slow look brings it back. **Don't remind me** turns it off until a rate is picked again in Settings.
- **Performance log:** Settings → Export performance log downloads a JSON file. It holds the frame rate of each look played, still-frame draw times, page-switch frame gaps, and the device and GPU, with the slowest looks summarized first. The last 300 entries are kept in the browser.
- **Load guard:** a design too heavy for the device can't lock anyone out. If the last visit froze or crashed while starting, the saved design opens without effects or objects, and a note offers to restore them. Designs with effects are also test-drawn small before the first frame, and one that would take more than three seconds opens the same way.
- Leaving the Studio pauses motion on its current frame, so the page change runs smoothly, and it resumes when you come back.
- **Smoothness first:** animations and transitions get the frames before anything else. Preview thumbnails draw only in idle time, wait while the pointer moves, the page scrolls or something animates, and stop while the Studio is off screen. Pages you leave stay laid out, so coming back only has to show them, and the Components page keeps what it built when nothing changed. The PNG file size in Export is estimated from a small sample instead of rendering the full image. Glass bars redraw their refraction once a resize settles, and Surprise tests its candidates one per task so frames keep drawing. Putting on the Caddis preset brings a note linking to caddis.app.
- **Version:** the bottom of settings shows the version and build date. Each deploy stamps a build id and busts the cache, and the app checks for a newer build when you come back to the tab.
- **Builder motion:** appear and disappear play on load or on scroll. On scroll, the CSS export runs the same keyframes on a scroll timeline.

- `glass.js`: liquid glass bars.

- `worker/feedback.js`: optional Cloudflare Worker that files feedback as GitHub issues.
